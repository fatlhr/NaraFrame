# General Explainer

Teach one idea from a stable subject: how something works, what the numbers
say, how two things differ. Nothing here depends on today's news, so this
profile deliberately carries none of the current-affairs rules.

- Show the visual question in the first three seconds. Put the object in an
  unusual state or show the consequence first. Let the action answer it once.
- Facts still need a source when they are specific, but dated sourcing is not
  required: the subject does not move.
- Choose a palette that fits the subject and keeps the important state readable.
  Carry the chosen look through the episode.
- Compare with structure rather than colour: put the two things in the same
  grid, at the same size, and let the difference show.
- Motion explains sequence. Reveal in the order the viewer should read, and
  hold still once everything is on screen.
- Use cause-and-effect staging when changing one part explains the result.
  Use editorial collage when assembling and removing layers makes a metaphor
  clear.
