# Current Issues

Explain a current event with factual, restrained, high-contrast editorial motion graphics optimized for vertical reading.

- Lead with the immediate event, then add only the historical context needed to understand it.
- Separate confirmed facts from estimates or developing information.
- Prefer short Indonesian sentences, strong hierarchy, and one visual idea per scene.
- Use warm paper surfaces, near-black backgrounds, and orange-red accents sparingly.
- Choose motion from the scene's meaning: use a sustained focus for maps or photos, stagger related composition layers, and repeat emphasis only where the viewer should look again.
- Keep movement smooth and readable at normal playback. Vary choreography by context instead of applying one entrance pattern to every scene.
- Write a short style brief and shot rundown before authoring scenes. Give each shot one dominant statement and one focal visual.
- Use display, heading, body, and label roles consistently. Shorten copy before shrinking it below the profile minimum.
- Review entry, settled, beat-peak, transition, and final frames. Reject card-grid pacing when the story needs a continuous camera move or visual through-line.
- Avoid sensational imagery, decorative motion, and claims that cannot be sourced.
